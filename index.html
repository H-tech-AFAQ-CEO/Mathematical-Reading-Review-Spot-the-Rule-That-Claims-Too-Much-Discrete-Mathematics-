SCREENING TASK · LOGIC & PROOF READING RULES

Verification of reading rules against Levin's Discrete Mathematics

Eight rules, each checked word-for-word against its source quotation. Verdict, reasoning, and — where the rule fails — the shortest fix.

Prepared byAfaq Ahmad—for client review
8 rules checked 3 confirmed (C) 5 flagged (F)
01F — flagged

In a proof by cases using Q and not‑Q, the two cases need not cover every possibility; each case only has to lead to P.

"…prove that P is true by proving that Q ⇒ P and ¬Q ⇒ P for some statement Q. So no matter what, whether or not Q is true, we know that P is true."

WHYQ and ¬Q are exhaustive by the law of excluded middle — that's precisely what lets the quote conclude "no matter what." The rule claims coverage is optional; the quote relies on it being total.

Fix —Q and ¬Q necessarily cover every possibility; what's flexible is only how each case reaches P.
02F — flagged

'P is sufficient for Q' means that Q implies P.

"P is sufficient for Q means P ⇒ Q."

WHYThe quote states the direction plainly: P ⇒ Q. The rule reverses it to Q ⇒ P.

Fix —'P is sufficient for Q' means P ⇒ Q, not Q ⇒ P.
03C — confirmed

Proving the converse alone does not, in general, prove the original implication.

"The converse is NOT logically equivalent to the original implication."

WHYNon-equivalence of converse and original is exactly the textual support the rule needs.

04F — flagged

A sentence with a free variable becomes a statement only when the variable is given a specific value; quantifying over it is not enough.

"Or you could capture the free variable by quantifying over it, as in, for all values of x, 3+x = 12, which is false."

WHYThe quote uses quantification itself to produce a sentence with a definite truth value — the opposite of "not enough."

Fix —A free variable becomes a statement either by assigning it a specific value or by quantifying over it.
05F — flagged

'P only if Q' means that Q implies P.

"…is equivalent to saying if I sing, then I'm in the shower, so the only if part is P ⇒ Q."

WHYThe quote names the direction explicitly: P ⇒ Q, not Q ⇒ P.

Fix —'P only if Q' means P ⇒ Q.
06C — confirmed

One counterexample is enough to show that a universal statement is false.

"Since so many statements in mathematics are universal, making their negations existential, we can often prove that a statement is false … by providing a counterexample."

WHYAn existential negation is witnessed by a single instance — the quote's stated route to disproof.

07F — flagged

An implication whose hypothesis is false is itself false.

"…an implication is true either when the hypothesis is false, or when the conclusion is true."

WHYA false hypothesis is listed as one of the two conditions that make the implication true — the reverse of the rule.

Fix —An implication with a false hypothesis is vacuously true, not false.
08C — confirmed

In this definition, the contrapositive reverses the roles of P and Q and negates both.

"The contrapositive of an implication P ⇒ Q is the statement ¬Q ⇒ ¬P."

WHY¬Q ⇒ ¬P swaps which letter leads and negates both — matching "reverses… and negates both" exactly.
